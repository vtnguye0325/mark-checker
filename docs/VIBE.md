# Spectrum dial vibe

## Purpose

Mark Checker should feel like an instrument that helps a person make sense of one uncertain finding.
The interface should invite a careful read and make the next step clear.

Use the [design principles](DESIGN_PRINCIPLES.md) for exact rules.
Use the [start mockup](mockups/bleibtgleich26/b-spectrum-dial.html) and [result mockup](mockups/bleibtgleich26/b-spectrum-dial-result.html) for composition.

## Scene

Picture a quiet desk with one precise object on it.
The olive field gives the dial its space.
The paper field gives the evidence room to breathe.
Dark type and thin rules show order without visual noise.

The first screen should make the five-part spectrum easy to explore.
The result screen should make the actual mark and finding easy to see.
The dial should support the answer, not hide it.

## Tone

Use plain, direct words for the owner who has no legal training.
Define legal terms near their first use.
Use measured words when the model has uncertainty.
Call the result a first read and the number a model score.

Use short labels for controls and states.
Use full sentences for limits, errors, and next steps.
Keep the voice calm when a request fails.

## Details that make the direction distinct

- Use one five-part dial for the landing lesson and the real result.
- Let the mark name carry visual weight in the result.
- Use small numbered marks only when they help the reader locate a category.
- Use ruled rows for sources and score details.
- Give the result a clear path from finding to reason to source.
- Use one small round symbol as a quiet brand cue.

## Limits

Do not make the dial look like a gauge of legal certainty.
Do not imply that a model score gives the chance of registration.
Do not fill empty states with sample evidence.
Do not use motion or ornament to make a weak result seem strong.
Do not let display type reduce the legibility of the evidence.

The final build must resolve these points with real content and failure states.
